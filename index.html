Can EU gigafactories supply the cells the mandate would require?

Cell demand from EVs sold in the EU under three Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA) design options, set against the EU production capacity that gigafactory projects are expected to deliver by 2027 and 2030.

Each panel is a target year. Bars stack cell demand by where the cells would be sourced; the dashed horizontal lines mark cumulative EU production capacity by project confidence level.

The headline

Total demand is fixed in each year — 427 GWh/yr in 2027 and 810 GWh/yr in 2030. What the IAA changes is not how many cells Europe needs, but how many of them must be made in Europe. That is what turns a demand curve into an industrial-capacity test.

2027 is already tight

A strict EU scope would pull EU-sourced demand to 290 GWh/yr. High-confidence gigafactory capacity covers only 258 GWh/yr. The gap closes only if medium-confidence projects (311 GWh/yr) come online on schedule — leaving essentially no buffer for delay.

2030 hinges on the middle tier

By 2030 a strict EU scope implies 550 GWh/yr of EU-sourced demand against just 397 GWh/yr of high-confidence capacity — a shortfall of roughly 150 GWh/yr. Adding medium-confidence projects lifts supply to 571 GWh/yr, which clears the bar by a mere 4%. The mandate is therefore feasible, but only on the assumption that the medium-confidence pipeline delivers in full.

Scope design is the lever

Widening eligibility to EU+FTA partners cuts EU-scope demand from 550 to 523 GWh/yr in 2030 and shifts 27 GWh/yr into an undetermined category that may or may not land in Europe. It buys headroom against the supply constraint at the cost of weaker guarantees that the investment actually happens on EU soil.

Read with care

Demand covers light-duty vehicles only — heavy-duty transport and stationary storage are excluded. Including them would raise demand materially and widen every gap shown here. The no-IAA baseline assumes today's EU origin share simply holds, with no further outsourcing.

Source: Demand from T&E's IAA impact assessment model, assuming all EU-assembled BEV+PHEV (personal & commercial) under a subsidy scheme become compliant; heavy-duty and stationary storage excluded. The no-IAA scenario assumes the current EU origin share of cells remains constant, without further outsourcing. Supply from T&E's Gigafactory ramp-up model, limited to EU scope.
